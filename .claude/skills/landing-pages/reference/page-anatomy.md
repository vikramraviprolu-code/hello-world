# Landing page anatomy — section by section

A high-converting product page is a sequence, each section doing one job and
earning the scroll to the next. Order matters: it mirrors how a skeptical
first-time visitor decides.

## 1. Hero / above the fold (the 5-second test)

The only section every visitor sees. It must answer *what is this, is it for
me, what do I do next* before any scroll. Components:

- **Headline** — the single most compelling end-benefit, stated plainly.
  Benefit or outcome, not feature or category. Clear beats clever.
- **Subheadline** — one or two lines: who it's for + how it delivers the
  benefit + the key differentiator. This is where specificity lives.
- **Primary CTA** — one action, visually dominant, action-worded ("Start
  free", not "Submit"). Secondary CTA (e.g. "See how it works") may sit
  beside it, visually quieter.
- **Hero visual** — show the product doing the thing (screenshot, short
  loop, diagram). Not a stock photo of smiling people.
- **Trust strip** — customer logos or a headline metric ("12,000 teams"),
  if real. Immediate credibility.

Failure mode: a clever headline that names no benefit. If a stranger can't
say what you sell after reading the hero, rewrite it.

## 2. Value proposition (why it matters)

Expand the hero's promise into 2–4 concrete benefits. Each: a benefit-led
subhead + one or two sentences of proof/specificity. Lead with the outcome,
then explain the mechanism — never the reverse. Tie each benefit to a real
user pain.

## 3. How it works (make it feel easy)

3–4 steps, each a short verb phrase. Purpose: collapse perceived effort and
risk of adopting. Show the product at each step. If onboarding is genuinely
one command / one click, say so here — low friction is itself a selling
point.

## 4. Social proof (de-risk the decision)

People trust people. Order by persuasive weight for your audience:
- Named testimonials with role + company + a specific result ("cut our
  build time 40%") beat anonymous praise.
- Recognizable logos.
- Hard metrics (users, uptime, ratings) — only real ones.
- Case studies / third-party reviews for high-consideration purchases.

Place proof near the decisions it supports (a testimonial about ease-of-use
next to "How it works"; an ROI stat next to pricing).

## 5. Objection handling (remove the last blockers)

Preempt the top reasons this audience would NOT convert — price, migration
cost, security, lock-in, learning curve. A focused FAQ or a comparison table
does this well. Answer honestly; a dodged objection converts worse than an
addressed one. (This section also feeds `geo` — honest comparison tables get
cited by AI engines.)

## 6. Final CTA (close)

Restate the core benefit in one line and repeat the primary CTA. The visitor
who scrolled this far is warm — don't make them scroll back up to act. One
CTA goal, no competing links pulling attention away.

## Page-level rules

- **One page, one conversion goal.** Competing CTAs split intent and lower
  both. Secondary actions must be visually subordinate.
- **Momentum:** every section should answer the question the previous one
  raised and provoke the next. If a section can be deleted without the
  visitor missing it, delete it.
- **Scannability:** benefit-led subheads carry the argument for skimmers;
  body copy rewards those who stop. Most visitors skim — the subheads alone
  should sell.
